# CRM V0.1 Design

## Goal

Use one customer aggregate to manage the path from initial recognition through payment: customer, follow-up, opportunity, quotation, contract, and payment.

## Scope

- Customer is the only pre-sale customer entity. `poolStatus` represents `private` or `public`.
- Contacts and follow-ups belong to a customer. Follow-ups retain type, content, contact, occurred time, next follow-up time, creator, attachments, and extensible metadata.
- Opportunities, quotations, contracts, payments, products/services, tasks, and attachments are customer-related objects.
- Public pool supports listing, searching, filtering, claiming, assignment, and returning customers.
- Detail interactions use the existing right-side Drawer pattern. Customer detail tabs are Overview, Journey, Contacts, Opportunities, Quotations, Contracts, Payments, Tasks, and Attachments.

## Explicit Exclusions

- No lead or lead-pool UI, API, permission, data model, or menu.
- No visit plan, ticket, expense, invoice, approval, inventory, procurement, receivable reconciliation, write-off, BI engine, automation, or complex RBAC.
- No migration compatibility for `crm_lead` data. The environment contains test data and permits destructive schema upgrades.

## Data Rules

- `crm_customer` is retained and extended where required; its owner and `pool_status` form the public-pool model.
- Drop `crm_lead` and `crm_lead_activity` during the destructive migration.
- Replace payment-plan/write-off complexity with a single payment record model. `receivedAmount` is the sum of actual payment amounts, and `remainingAmount` is contract amount minus received amount.
- Status metadata lives in one domain constants module with value, label, and semantic token. UI components render these mappings rather than choosing status colors locally.

## Customer Lifecycle

Customer status separates a salesperson's relationship judgment from commercial facts. This prevents a generic customer edit from changing reporting facts or bypassing the sales process.

### State Model

`relationshipStatus` is explicitly maintained by a salesperson:

```text
potential | following | lost
```

`statusCode` is the effective, system-projected status rendered by the UI:

```text
potential | following | opportunity | customer | lost
```

Projection precedence is deterministic:

```text
relationshipStatus = lost                         -> lost
performing or completed contract exists           -> customer
active or won-pending-contract opportunity exists -> opportunity
otherwise                                         -> relationshipStatus
```

An opportunity stage represents a specific sale. A customer status represents the broader customer relationship. Therefore, a won opportunity without a qualifying completion fact remains `opportunity`, not `customer`.

### Normal and Exceptional Close Paths

The normal B2B close path is:

```text
won opportunity -> quotation optional -> contract performing/completed -> customer
```

For legitimate low-value, informal, repeat-purchase, or self-service transactions that do not create a contract, a won opportunity may use a controlled **confirm close without contract** action:

```text
won opportunity -> confirmed direct close -> customer
```

This is not a freely editable customer status. It must be launched from a won opportunity and creates a `directClose` commercial fact with:

```text
amount, closedAt, evidenceType, attachment or remark, ownerId
```

Supported evidence types are `payment_proof`, `order_confirmation`, `verbal_confirmation`, and `other`. The action and any reversal require a reason and write a system activity. A later contract can be linked to the same opportunity; reporting must not double-count the direct-close amount and contract amount.

The effective status projection treats a valid direct close as `customer`, at the same precedence as a performing/completed contract. Contract-level receivables remain contract-based. A payment against an informal close is linked to its direct-close record and is visibly labeled as a no-contract payment; this avoids inventing a contract record or an order subsystem.

### Allowed Transitions and Guardrails

The customer Drawer displays the primary path:

```text
potential -> following -> opportunity -> customer     lost
```

`lost` is a separate terminal branch, not the next step after `customer`.

- Salespeople may move `potential` and `following` in either direction as a relationship judgment.
- A first valid follow-up promotes `potential` to `following`.
- Creating an active opportunity projects the customer to `opportunity`.
- Losing or deleting the final opportunity restores the stored relationship status; it never automatically marks the customer lost.
- A draft or terminated contract never produces `customer`.
- A performing/completed contract, or valid direct close, produces `customer`.
- Marking a customer lost requires a reason and is blocked when active opportunities, qualifying contracts, or valid direct closes exist.
- Reactivating a lost customer moves its relationship status to `following` and requires a remark.
- A lost customer cannot create an opportunity, quotation, contract, direct close, or payment until reactivated.
- A customer with a qualifying commercial fact cannot be manually downgraded to potential, following, or lost.
- Generic customer PATCH cannot write either `relationshipStatus` or `statusCode`.

### Interface and Audit

Customer relationship changes use a dedicated transition endpoint rather than generic editing:

```http
POST /crm/v1/customers/:id/relationship-status-transitions
```

Every transition runs in one transaction: validate data scope and preconditions, persist the relationship change, recompute the effective status, and create a `crm_activity` record with `type = status_change`. The activity metadata records `from`, `to`, `reasonCode`, `remark`, and `source`.

Opportunity and contract mutations that change lifecycle facts call the same lifecycle projector. They do not update `statusCode` directly. The UI shows why a projected state applies and links to the relevant opportunity, contract, or direct-close record; it does not offer a misleading direct edit for `opportunity` or `customer`.

### Test-data Migration

The test-data upgrade may be destructive. Existing values migrate as follows before projection:

```text
potential -> potential
following -> following
lost -> lost
opportunity/customer -> following
```

The projector then recomputes `statusCode` from extant opportunities, contracts, and direct-close records. Lead tables and activities remain removed as specified above.

## Information Architecture

- Home
- Customer management: Customers, Public Pool, Contacts
- Sales management: Opportunities, Quotations, Contracts, Payments
- Workbench: Tasks, Products/Services
- Mobile tabs remain Home, Customers, Workbench, Mine. Workbench exposes the eight CRM actions.

## Acceptance Paths

1. A salesperson creates a potential customer, contact, follow-up, and next follow-up reminder.
2. A customer has an opportunity advanced through stages, a quotation, and a contract created from that quotation.
3. A 100000 contract with payments of 20000 and 30000 reports received 50000 and remaining 50000.
4. A private customer is returned to public pool, claimed by another salesperson, and shown in that salesperson's customer list.
5. No user-facing lead or lead-pool route or menu remains.
